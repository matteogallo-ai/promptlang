# System card: the AI linter

`promptlang analyze --ai` sends each prompt of the given `.prompt` files to
Claude and reports the defects it finds, next to the six deterministic rules of
`promptlang analyze`.

## Intended use

A second reader for prompt text, before review: it points at missing output
formats, ambiguous instructions, contradictions and similar defects
(`evals/linter/labels.json` lists the categories). A person reads each finding
and decides.

## Out of scope

- Gating a merge or a release on its findings alone: they are model output,
  with false alarms and misses.
- Judging whether a prompt is safe against injection: the static rule
  `prompt-injection-risk` checks the one thing that is computable, and nothing
  measures the linter on attacks (`docs/THREAT_MODEL.md`, LLM01).
- Prompts in languages other than English: the corpus is English only.

## Data

The linter sends the text of each prompt, inside `<untrusted_prompt>` delimiters,
to the Anthropic API, and nothing else from the project. Anthropic's terms
govern that text; do not lint prompts that hold secrets or personal data. The
eval corpus is 120 synthetic cases written for the purpose
(`evals/linter/README.md`): 30 base tasks, each clean and with three single-defect
variants, split dev/test by base task so no task appears in both.

## Measured performance

Precision is over the categories the linter reported; a reported category that
was not designed into the case counts as a false positive, so precision is a
lower bound when a case has a defect nobody designed. Each proportion is given
with its numerator, denominator and Wilson 95% interval; two repeats of the same
run show the noise between runs.

## Known limits

- It over-reports: on the measured run it names several categories for almost
  every prompt, clean ones included (table above). Its findings are leads for a
  reviewer, which is why gating on them is out of scope.
- Precision counts every undesigned category as a false positive, and some may
  be real defects the generator put in by accident. The labels' human check
  (`evals/linter/human-review/`, one annotator, 30 of 120 cases) confirmed 27/30
  designed labels (90%, Wilson 95% CI 74.4%–96.5%) and rejected 27/30 decoys
  (90%, same interval), kappa 0.80. Two of the three accepted decoys share one
  cause: in 7 of the 15 hallucination cases, 2 of them on the test split, the
  instruction to fill every missing value contradicts the rule that a boolean is
  false unless shown. The decoys are drawn at random rather than from the
  linter's findings, so the check does not measure precision; the figures above
  remain lower bounds.
- The corpus is synthetic and small: 45 defects per split, so an overall recall
  interval is up to ±14 points wide, and 7 or 8 per category per split, so the
  per-category figures separate only large differences.
- One model (`claude-haiku-5-5`) at the API's default temperature; another model
  needs its own run.
- Labels are the defects each case was built with; a defect present by
  accident is scored as a false positive.
- A failed call leaves a case without a verdict; it is reported, not counted as
  clean or as a miss.
